# Requirements: discovery, universal must-haves, legal

## 1. Discovery questions

Ask at most 5 at once, only the ones whose answers change the build. Polish phrasing
examples in brackets.

- What do you sell / offer, and at what price level? („Co dokładnie oferujesz i w jakich cenach?”)
- Who is the customer and where? („Kto jest klientem i skąd — miasto, region, cała Polska?”)
- What should a visitor do on the site? („Co ma zrobić osoba na stronie: zadzwonić, wysłać formularz, kupić, zarezerwować?”)
- What do you already have? („Masz logo, zdjęcia, teksty, domenę, starą stronę?”)
- Who will update content and how often? („Kto będzie zmieniał treści i ceny — jak często?”)
- Competitors or sites you like/dislike? („Jakie strony konkurencji lub inne strony Ci się podobają, a jakie nie?”)
- Hard constraints: hosting, technology, deadline, budget.

## 2. Universal must-haves — every website

Structure and navigation
- Clear header: logo (link to home), main navigation (≤ 7 items), primary CTA visible.
- Mobile navigation that works with one thumb; sticky header if the page is long.
- Hero that says in one sentence what it is, for whom, and what to do next.
- Footer: contact details, address/area, hours, legal links, social links, copyright.
- 404 page with a way back; breadcrumbs on deep structures.

Content and trust
- Concrete offer (services/products with prices or price ranges where the market allows).
- Social proof: real reviews/testimonials, ratings, case studies, client logos (only real).
- Who is behind it: about section, people/team photo, company data (NIP, address).
- Contact: click-to-call phone, e-mail, form, map (for local), response time promise.
- FAQ answering the real objections (price, time, guarantee, process, payment).

Technical
- HTTPS, fast loading (Core Web Vitals: LCP < 2.5 s, INP < 200 ms, CLS < 0.1).
- Responsive from 360 px, accessible (WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast, keyboard, alt, labels).
- SEO basics: unique `<title>`/meta description per page, one H1, semantic headings,
  clean URLs, sitemap.xml, robots.txt, canonical, Open Graph/Twitter cards, favicon set,
  schema.org structured data for the site type.
- Analytics (privacy-friendly or consent-gated), Google Search Console ready,
  conversion events (form sent, call click, purchase).
- Form protection: server validation, honeypot/captcha, rate limiting, success and
  error states, confirmation e-mail where useful.
- Security headers and no secrets in the frontend.
- Polish characters (ą ć ę ł ń ó ś ź ż) render correctly in every font and weight.

## 3. Conversion basics

- One primary action per page, repeated at natural decision points (after offer,
  after reviews, at the end).
- Remove friction: short forms (ask only what's needed), autofill, clear errors, no
  surprise costs.
- Answer "why you" with specifics (time, guarantee, equipment, experience in numbers
  that are true).
- Show process ("Jak to działa: 1-2-3"), pricing logic, and what happens after contact.
- Make contact possible from every screen on mobile (sticky call/WhatsApp/booking bar
  for local services).

## 4. Legal and compliance — Poland / EU

Every site
- **Polityka prywatności** (RODO/GDPR): administrator, purposes, legal bases, recipients
  (hosting, analytics, mailing), retention, rights, contact.
- **Cookies/consent**: analytics and marketing tags only after consent, equal
  "Akceptuj"/"Odrzuć" prominence, withdrawable; Google Consent Mode v2 if Google tags
  are used.
- Forms: information clause, unticked consent checkboxes for marketing, separate
  newsletter consent (double opt-in recommended).
- Company data (name, address, NIP/KRS where applicable) — required for businesses
  and builds trust.
- **European Accessibility Act** (applies since 28 June 2025 to e-commerce and many
  consumer services; micro-enterprises providing services are exempt) — build to
  WCAG 2.2 AA anyway, and add an accessibility statement for shops.

Online shops (consumer sales)
- **Regulamin sklepu**: ordering, payment, delivery, complaints (reklamacje),
  withdrawal.
- **Prawo odstąpienia** 14 days, model withdrawal form, information on return costs.
- Total price with VAT and delivery costs visible before the order; order button with
  an unambiguous label ("Zamawiam i płacę" style — obligation to pay must be clear).
- **Omnibus**: when showing a discount, show the lowest price from the 30 days before
  the discount; state whether and how reviews are verified; fake reviews prohibited.
- **GPSR** (General Product Safety Regulation, since 13 Dec 2024): product listings show
  manufacturer/responsible person data and safety information/warnings where relevant.
- Invoices on request (NIP field at checkout for B2B). **KSeF**: issuing structured
  invoices through KSeF is mandatory for B2B invoices for most companies since 1 April
  2026 (largest since 1 Feb 2026, micro-businesses up to 10 000 zł/month in sales from
  1 Jan 2027); for B2C it is optional — check current rules before relying on this.
- Online dispute resolution info if still required for the business; check current
  rules (the EU ODR platform was being phased out in 2025).

Other
- Services with bookings: cancellation terms, deposit rules.
- Restaurants: allergens info for the menu (EU rules on food information).
- Alcohol, supplements, medical, financial, children's products: sector rules —
  flag them to the user, don't guess.

This is a checklist for building the right pages and components, not legal advice;
say so and suggest the user confirms the texts (regulamin, polityka) with a lawyer or a
reputable generator.

## 5. Market research procedure

When web tools are available:

1. Search for the niche + location in the market language
   (e.g. "mycie kostki brukowej Gdańsk", "sklep z kawą speciality", "fryzjer Kraków").
   Take the top organic results and the Google Maps leaders with many reviews.
2. Add 1–2 category leaders nationally and 1–2 world-class international benchmarks.
3. For each site, record: sections present, primary CTA, pricing transparency, trust
   elements, booking/checkout flow, special tools (calculator, configurator, quiz,
   before/after, live availability), content (blog, guides), speed impression.
4. Build a feature matrix: rows = features, columns = sites. Features on ≥ 70 % of
   leaders are **table stakes** (must be in every option). Features only the best 1–3
   have are **differentiators** (candidates for "Propozycje spoza Twojej listy").
5. Cross-check trends against evidence; ignore fads that hurt usability (scroll-jacking,
   autoplay video with sound, splash screens).

Report the sources briefly (site names) in the proposal so the user can see the
recommendations are grounded.
